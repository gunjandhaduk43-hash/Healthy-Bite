Project Overview

I want to build a complete web-based SaaS platform called Healthy Bite – Digital Menu and Food Ordering System.

Healthy Bite is a QR-code-based digital menu and food ordering platform for restaurants, cafés, hotels, food courts, cloud kitchens, and similar food businesses.

The platform should allow multiple restaurants to register and manage their own digital menus while customers can simply scan a QR code placed on their table to browse the menu and place orders without waiting for a waiter.

Unlike existing QR menu systems, Healthy Bite focuses on healthy eating by displaying complete nutritional information for every food item, including calories, protein, carbohydrates, fats, ingredients, allergens, preparation time, and dietary type (Veg, Non-Veg, Vegan, Jain).

The website should be modern, responsive, secure, user-friendly, and scalable.

IMPORTANT SECURITY FEATURE

Most QR ordering systems have a security problem.

Example:

Bad URL:

/menu.php?restaurant=15&table=5

Customer can change:

table=5

to

table=8

and place an order on another customer's table.

Healthy Bite must NEVER allow this.

Instead:

Generate a random unique QR token for every table.

Example:

/menu.php?id=HBT9X82LPAQ7M

Store this token in the database.

When QR is scanned:

Find the token.

Identify restaurant.

Identify table.

Create a secure PHP session.

All future orders use the session.

Never trust the table number from the browser URL.

If someone edits the URL manually:

Show

"Invalid QR Code"

This security feature is mandatory.

Why 69?
Commercial restaurant SaaS platforms typically separate concepts like allergens, dietary tags, payment transactions, and activity logs into their own tables. This keeps the database normalized, avoids duplicate data, and makes reporting much easier. Some simple college projects merge these together, but that leads to redundancy and harder maintenance.

I want to improve this before we go further
After laying this out, I see an opportunity to make the project even more realistic.

Rather than immediately listing every column, I recommend refining the architecture first. Some tables above are candidates to merge (for example, chef_recommendations can be represented with flags on food_items), while others should be split further depending on whether you want enterprise-level features.

So, before we define columns and relationships, I'd like to produce a Version 3.0 database architecture with approximately 60–70 carefully designed tables instead of 85. It will be cleaner, easier to implement in PHP/MySQL, and still support every feature you've requested.

I believe that will give you a stronger foundation for your ER diagram and implementation than simply maximizing the number of tables.
